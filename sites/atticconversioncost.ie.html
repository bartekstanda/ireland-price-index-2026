3. Direct-Answer Consumer FAQs (Attic Conversion Cost)

How much does an attic conversion cost in Ireland in 2026?

A non-habitable storage conversion costs €15,000 to €22,000. A full habitable bedroom or study with roof windows (Velux), a fixed fire-protected stairs and Building Regulations sign-off costs €20,000 to €35,000. A rear or side dormer conversion runs €28,000 to €45,000, and a hip-to-gable conversion on a semi-detached house costs €35,000 to €55,000 or more. Adding an en-suite shower room adds €5,000 to €8,000.

Do I need planning permission for a dormer attic conversion in 2026?

Since 27 July 2026, a dormer to the side or rear of a house is exempt from planning permission under Class 1B of S.I. No. 344 of 2026, provided all dormers on the house together do not exceed 30 cubic metres internally, it sits no higher than the roof ridge, and it is set back at least 20 cm from the edge of the roof. Front dormers, hip-to-gable extensions and raising the ridge still require planning permission.

Are front Velux roof windows exempt from planning permission?

Yes, within the new 2026 limits. Under Class 1C of S.I. No. 344 of 2026 (in force 27 July 2026), up to two roof windows on the existing front roof slope are exempt from planning permission, provided each window is no larger than 100 cm by 90 cm. Rear roof windows are generally exempt under Section 4(1)(h) of the Planning Act.

What is the difference between a storage conversion and a habitable bedroom?

A storage conversion (€15,000 to €22,000) boards, insulates and lights the attic, often with a folding ladder or basic stairs, but skips the fire-protected stairwell, self-closing fire doors and structural floor upgrades required for sleeping rooms. When you sell the house, a storage attic cannot legally be advertised or valued by a bank as an extra bedroom.

What is the 2.4 metre ceiling height rule for Irish attics?

Technical Guidance Document F (Ventilation, Diagram 3) sets out a suggested ceiling height for a habitable room in a roof space: the area at least 2.4 m high should equal at least half the floor area that has 1.5 m or more of headroom. While it is ventilation guidance rather than planning law, buyers' surveyors check it closely during conveyancing.

Do attic conversion prices include VAT?

Many builders quote ex-VAT at first. Attic conversion building work qualifies for the 13.5% reduced construction VAT rate under Revenue's two-thirds rule. Always ask for a written quote confirming that 13.5% VAT is included.
