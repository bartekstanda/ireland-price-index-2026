3. Direct-Answer Consumer FAQs (Composite Gates)

How much do composite gates cost in Ireland?

A composite side gate costs €400–€500 as a kit you fit yourself, or €1,350–€1,550 made to measure. A composite driveway pair is €600–€950 for the gate alone, and an electric swing gate, supplied and fitted, €2,500–€3,500.

What is a composite gate?

A composite gate has a frame, usually aluminium or galvanised steel, filled with composite boards: a wood-plastic mix that looks like timber but isn't painted or stained, and doesn't rot or splinter.

Composite, timber or metal?

Composite needs the least upkeep: no painting or staining, and the aluminium frame doesn't rust. Timber is cheaper to buy but needs treating every year or two. Steel is strongest but rusts where the coating is broken.

Do I need planning permission for a composite gate?

Usually not. Gates up to 2 metres high at a house are exempted development under Class 5 of the Planning and Development Regulations 2001. A fence beside it in front of the house is limited to 1.2 metres, and a new or wider entrance onto a public road needs permission.

How long does it take?

A kit or a stock-size gate can go up in a day once the posts or piers are sound. A made-to-measure gate is built to your opening first. New piers, a track or a cable run add days for the concrete to set.

Can a composite gate be electric?

Yes. A composite pair or a sliding gate can take a motor: €1,200–€3,500+ on a gate you have, or €2,500–€3,500 for electric swing gates supplied and fitted. Solid boards catch the wind, so the motor has to be sized for it.
