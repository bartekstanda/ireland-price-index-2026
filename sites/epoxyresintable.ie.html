3. Direct-Answer Consumer FAQs (Epoxy Resin Table)

How much does an epoxy resin table cost in Ireland?

An Irish-made resin coffee table costs €450–€550. A live edge dining table for 6 in oak is €1,800–€3,200 before resin, and a 2.5 m river dining table in Irish timber with resin €4,000–€5,100.

Why are resin tables so expensive?

The slab, the resin and the time. A wide, dry hardwood slab is scarce; a river can take 10 litres of casting resin or more; and the resin goes in layers that each have to cure before the top is flattened, sanded and finished.

Does epoxy resin go yellow?

All epoxy yellows a little in strong sunlight over time: no epoxy is fully UV resistant. Table resins carry UV absorbers, and a polyurethane topcoat helps. Keep the table out of strong direct sun and it holds its look for years indoors.

Can I put a hot plate on a resin table?

No. Cured table resin starts to soften at about 40 to 60°C, so a hot pan, a hot plate or a mug straight from the kettle can mark it. Use mats and coasters. A mark in the resin can be sanded and polished out.

How long does a resin table take to make?

The making is 2 to 3 days for a simple slab table and 5 to 8 days for complex work. Resin adds its own time: each pour waits 12 to 72 hours before the next, and a deep pour needs 7 to 14 days to fully cure.

Can I see the slab before it's made?

Yes, and you should. A good maker sends photos or has you in to pick the slab, because no two are alike and the grain decides the look of the whole table.
