3. Direct-Answer Consumer FAQs (Gastric Band Ireland)

Do surgeons in Ireland still fit new gastric bands?

Very rarely. Adjustable gastric bands now account for under 1% of bariatric operations internationally because long-term rates of slippage, gullet stretching, reflux and band removal proved high. Most Irish patients looking for surgery compare a keyhole gastric sleeve (€6,500 to €10,000) or gastric bypass (€7,500 to €12,600), while those wanting a temporary option without incisions look at a gastric balloon (€3,400 to €4,900).

Can an Irish clinic help with a gastric band I already have?

Yes. Bariatric clinics in Ireland regularly look after people whose band was fitted years ago in Ireland, the UK or abroad. Depending on your symptoms, that can mean drawing fluid out of the port (deflation), a barium swallow X-ray, keyhole band removal, or converting the band to a sleeve or bypass.

How much does gastric band removal or conversion cost in Ireland?

Band removal and revisional conversion are quoted after assessment because the theatre time depends on scar tissue, tests beforehand, and whether conversion happens in one sitting or two stages. As a benchmark, first-time keyhole gastric sleeve packages in Ireland sit at €6,500 to €10,000 and gastric bypass packages sit at €7,500 to €12,600.

What are the warning signs of a slipped or over-tight band?

Night cough, waking with sour fluid in your throat, heartburn when lying flat, regurgitating solid food hours after eating, or finding you can only manage soft and liquid foods. If you cannot swallow liquids or have severe tummy or chest pain, go straight to a hospital emergency department.

Will I regain weight if my gastric band is removed?

Usually yes, if the band is simply taken out and nothing replaces it. Once the silicone ring and fibrous capsule are released, hunger and meal size return to normal. Surgeons discuss structured medical support or converting to a gastric sleeve or bypass when keeping weight off matters.

Who qualifies for bariatric surgery in Ireland?

Under HSE clinical guidance, bariatric surgery is considered when you are fit for anaesthetic, can commit to long-term follow-up, and have a BMI of 40 or over, or a BMI between 35 and 40 alongside a weight-related condition such as type 2 diabetes, sleep apnoea or high blood pressure. Lower thresholds can be considered for South Asian, Chinese, other Asian, Middle Eastern and Black ethnic backgrounds.
