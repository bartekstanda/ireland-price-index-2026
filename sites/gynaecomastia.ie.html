3. Direct-Answer Consumer FAQs (Gynaecomastia.ie)

What is gynaecomastia?

Gynaecomastia is breast gland tissue growing in a man or boy. It shows as a firm, rubbery disc under the nipple, on one or both sides, and it is often tender at first. It is not the same as chest fat, and it is very common: about half of teenage boys get some during puberty.

Is gynaecomastia the same as man boobs?

Not always. "Man boobs" is used for both true gynaecomastia (gland) and chest fat. Gland feels like a firm disc behind the nipple; fat is soft all over. Many men have a mix of the two.

Will it go away on its own?

Often, yes. In teenagers it usually settles within six months to two years. In adults it often settles once the cause is dealt with, such as a medicine, steroids or weight. Tissue that has been there for more than a year tends to turn fibrous and stays.

Can exercise get rid of gynaecomastia?

Exercise burns fat, so it helps when the chest is mostly fat. It can't shrink gland tissue, and chest workouts build muscle underneath, which can push the disc forward. If the firm disc is still there after you're lean, exercise won't move it.

When should I see a GP?

When it's new and you're an adult, when it's on one side, when it's painful, or when it's growing. See a GP soon for a hard or fixed lump, nipple discharge or blood, a pulled-in nipple, skin changes or a lump in the armpit.

Is surgery the answer?

For long-standing gland tissue in an adult whose weight is steady and whose GP has checked for a cause, yes, surgery is the treatment that removes it. In Ireland it costs €3,000 to €6,900 privately, our price guide. It isn't for teenagers who are still growing.
