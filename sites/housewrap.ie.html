3. Direct-Answer Consumer FAQs (House Wrap)

Do I need planning permission?

Usually not, since 27 July 2026. External wall insulation to a house is exempted development (Class 63, S.I. No. 341 of 2026) if it's no more than 150 mm thick on the front, rear or gable, doesn't encroach on a public road, and doesn't materially change how the house looks against its neighbours. If unsure, ask your council for a Section 5 declaration.

How long does it take?

Weeks rather than days, with scaffold up: most houses take 2 to 3 weeks, weather permitting, and NSAI says an average semi-D should be done within a fortnight. See how long it takes .

Can I stay in the house during the work?

Usually yes. The work is outside, though there's scaffold, noise and some disruption.

Will my windows look sunk in?

The wall gets thicker, so the windows sit deeper. New, longer sills and neat reveals keep it tidy.

Do I need an ESB Networks service alteration?

Maybe. SEAI says you may need one for external wall insulation, depending on how the cable reaches the house. Check with your contractor and ESB Networks before work starts.

Does the wrap change my BER?

It usually improves it, because the walls lose less heat. A post-works BER is part of the grant process.
