3. Direct-Answer Consumer FAQs (Root Canal Ireland)

How much does a root canal cost in Ireland?

A front tooth (incisor or canine) costs €390 to €700, a premolar €490 to €800, and a molar €590 to €1,250 (€900 to €1,250 with a specialist endodontist). If the tooth needs a crown afterwards, that adds €800 to €1,200. Both the root canal and the crown qualify for 20% tax relief on Form Med 2.

Can I claim 20% tax relief on a root canal and crown?

Yes. Root canal treatment (endodontics) and crowns are both listed by Revenue as qualifying non-routine dental treatments. Your dentist signs Form Med 2 when you pay, and you claim 20% back through Revenue myAccount under Health Expenses.

Does PRSI cover root canal treatment?

No. The Department of Social Protection's PRSI Treatment Benefit scheme covers an annual dental exam and €50 towards a scale and polish (or €42 towards periodontal treatment). It does not cover root canals, crowns or extractions.

Does the Medical Card cover a root canal?

Only on a front tooth. Under the HSE Dental Treatment Services Scheme (DTSS), a Medical Card covers one first-stage root canal on an incisor or canine per year. It does not cover premolars, molars or private crowns on back teeth.

Does a root canal hurt?

With modern local anaesthetic, a root canal should feel no different to having a deep filling placed. The procedure removes the inflamed or infected nerve that is causing your toothache. Mild tenderness when biting for 2 to 3 days afterwards is normal and settles with over-the-counter pain relief.

Do I always need a crown after a root canal?

Usually yes on a molar or premolar, and usually no on a front tooth. Back teeth take heavy chewing forces and become more brittle once hollowed out; a crown (€800 to €1,200) wraps the tooth so it does not split. Front teeth with intact walls are usually sealed with a tooth-coloured composite filling.
