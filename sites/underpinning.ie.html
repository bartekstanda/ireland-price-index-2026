3. Direct-Answer Consumer FAQs (Underpinning.ie)

What is underpinning?

Strengthening the foundation of an existing building by making it deeper or wider, so it rests on firmer ground or spreads its load over a bigger area. The main methods are mass concrete, beam and base, mini-piles and injection.

Does my house need underpinning?

Only a chartered structural engineer can say, after looking at the cracks, the ground and the drains. Most cracks don't mean underpinning: the crack scale treats cracks up to 5 mm as aesthetic.

What is the most common type of underpinning?

Mass concrete is the most common kind of underpinning, often applied to only part of a building.

How long does underpinning last?

There's no fixed number of years. Mass concrete underpinning is used where permanent support is needed. What matters later is that the cause was fixed, an engineer designed the work, and you keep the engineer's sign-off.

Can a wall be underpinned from the outside?

Yes, in many cases. A wall can often be underpinned from the outside, with a mini-piling machine small enough to go through a front door.

Do I need to move out during underpinning?

It depends on the method. A piled raft means taking up the internal floors. Work from outside the walls can leave the inside alone. Ask the engineer and the contractor before you agree a start date.
